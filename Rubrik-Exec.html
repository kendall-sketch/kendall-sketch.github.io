Intelligence Alert: Zero-Malware Threat

A Nation-State Attack That
Used No Malware.

In March 2026, a Fortune 500 healthcare company had more than 200,000 devices wiped across 79 countries — not by malware, but by its own cloud-based endpoint management platform turned against it.

The attack was carried out by a group linked to Iran's Ministry of Intelligence and Security. It was the first confirmed major cyberattack on a U.S. corporation following joint U.S.-Israeli military strikes on Iran beginning February 28, 2026.

A report from the Center for Strategic and International Studies found that the strikes were more likely to mark the beginning of a new phase of cyber escalation than its conclusion.

One Credential. One Console. Enterprise-Wide Destruction.

Attackers compromised a privileged admin account and used the company's Microsoft Intune console to issue remote wipe commands to every connected device. This "living off the land" technique required no custom malware — only one credential set with sufficient privilege in the right management plane.

Unit 42's 2026 report: 65% of initial access in recent incidents is identity-based. This was an identity governance and access control failure.

The Gaps This Attack Exploits

The architectural blind spots currently exploited by state-sponsored actors to dismantle global enterprise perimeters.

token

Identity Segregation

MDM admin accounts must be separated from daily-use accounts. One phished inbox should not carry device-wipe authority.

layers

Tier 0 Classification

MDM consoles control entire endpoint estates. They require dedicated admin accounts, no persistent sessions, and break-glass procedures stored offline.

query_stats

Anomaly Detection

Bulk remote wipe commands at unusual hours should trigger automatic holds requiring secondary approval.

devices

BYOD Enrollment

Containerized MAM-only profiles prevent corporate remote wipe commands from touching personal data on employee devices.

Executive Accountability Audit

Five questions every executive should be able to answer about every vendor with admin access.

1.

MFA Strength: Are they using phishing-resistant hardware keys for Tier 0 platform access?

2.

Access Residency: Is access granted just-in-time and revoked after each session?

3.

Anomalous Alerts: Are there active alerts for admin actions outside business hours or at unusual scale?

4.

Contractual Controls: What are the specific security requirements and breach liability terms?

5.

Verified Audit: When did you last pull sign-in logs to verify active accounts yourself?

Assess Your Exposure to Identity-Based Destructive Attacks

The gaps exploited in this attack exist across most enterprise environments. Identify Tier 0 vulnerabilities before they are weaponized.
